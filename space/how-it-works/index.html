How ADNS SPACE works

ADNS SPACE is a one-shot sampler. You load a short sample and play it on an X-Y pad. An arpeggiator plays that sample in your key and scale while you drag. The sound runs through ten effects you can put in any order, and a Saturator that always comes last. Six LFOs and two envelopes move the knobs. A sequencer of up to 64 steps plays its own notes, and any step can lock a knob or a switch to its own value. Rec saves what you play as audio and as automation, so a performance can be played again later with a different sample, key or scale.

It is a Mac app, for macOS 13 or later. There is no server and no account. Everything stays on your computer.

This page is a guide to the parts:

  1. The sample
  2. The X-Y pad and the arp
  3. The sequencer and parameter locks
  4. The effects
  5. LFOs and envelopes

1. The sample

Drop an audio file on the window, or press Load. The first 3 seconds are kept.

Drag inside the waveform and the nearest handle moves. The dashed ramps are short fades at each end of the trim. Rev reverses the sample. Norm brings its peak to −1 dBFS. A white playhead runs across the waveform on every note. Under the name, the length is the part you kept. A trimmed sample reads like 0.55 s of 0.90 s.

Root is the pitch of the sample, in octave 4. It starts at C. Set it to the note the sample actually is, and the arp plays that sample in tune: a higher note on the pad plays the sample faster and brighter, the way a sampler does.

Three readouts name different things. Root is the sample. Key and Scale on the arp name the notes the pad and the sequencer use, and they are the key and scale written into a take's file name. The corner of the pad shows the chord under the cursor, which changes as you drag.

2. The X-Y pad and the arp

Left to right picks the note in the chosen key and scale. The vertical lines are the notes, labelled along the bottom. Bottom to top picks the speed: 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32. The first note plays as soon as you touch the pad. Letting go stops it, unless Hold is on. Hold sits at the top of the pad. With it on, the arp keeps going after you lift your hand. The top right shows the chord or note, the speed and the pattern.

  • Scale: Major, Minor, Minor Pent, Major Pent, Dorian, Phrygian, Harm Minor, Chromatic. Key: C to B.
  • Octaves, from 1 to 4, sets how wide the pad is.
  • Pattern: Repeat plays the note under the cursor. Up, Down, Up-Dn and Rnd walk a chord built on that note. Chord is a triad or a 7th.
  • Gate is the note length, from 0 to 200% of a step. A full step, 100%, is the middle of the knob. At the very top the label is Ring: the sample rings until the next note cuts it.
  • Swing, from 0 to 60%, delays every second 1/8 or 1/16 note.

Gate and Swing apply to the sequencer as well. Notes are mono: each new note cuts off the one that is still sounding.

3. The sequencer and parameter locks

The sequencer sits at the bottom right. It has up to 64 steps, 16 on screen at a time, and it opens at 16. Page buttons 1 to 4 switch pages, and the page that is playing lights up. ▶ and ■ play and stop. The menu on the left sets the step length, from 1/4 to 1/32, starting at 1/16. The menu on the right sets the length of the pattern: 16, 32, 48 or 64 steps.

Click a step to switch it on or off. A step you switch on takes the note the pad is on, so put the pad on a note and then click the steps you want. Drag a step up or down, or scroll on it, to change its note inside the key and scale. Press and slide across steps to switch a row of them, the way you would run a finger along piano keys.

You can play the pad while the sequence runs. The two share one voice, so a pad note and a step cut each other off.

Lock gives a step its own setting, the way a parameter lock does on a step sequencer. Press Lock, then click a step. Turning an effect knob, or clicking an effect switch, writes that value onto the step. The knob or switch shows a small square, and the step shows a dot once it holds anything. A lock can be the on/off of an effect, the Saturator included, or a switch inside an effect: each Corpus resonator's On, each Filter delay line's On and Filt, and Inv on the Phaser-Flanger and the Auto pan. A Corpus or Filter delay tab lights up for the selected step's On.

  1. Hold Command (Ctrl on a keyboard without Command) and click more steps to select them together. A lock or an LFO map is written to every selected step. Command-click a selected step to drop it from the selection.
  2. Press Lock again, or Esc, to leave.

A lock takes effect at the start of its step, together with the note, and lasts until the next step starts. It applies on a step that is switched off too, so a silent step can still open an effect or move a knob for its own length. An effect switched on by a lock plays on that step only. The other steps, and the X-Y pad, keep the on/off you set by hand.

On the Filter delay, Space, Grain delay and Corpus, a lock on the on/off, on Mix, or on a line's Vol works like a send. It sets how much of that step's sound goes into the effect. The effect's output stays open while the effect is on, or while any step of the pattern locks it, so the echo, the reverb tail or the resonance keeps ringing after the step has ended. Locks on the sound of the effect itself, such as Time, Feedback, Freq, Pre-delay or Tune, last for that step only. With the effect on, turning Mix or Vol down leaves what is already inside it to ring out.

Space Size, Decay and Damp stay on the values you set. Changing them rebuilds the reverb, so a step keeps those three where you left them.

Double-click a knob while Lock is on to remove that knob's lock from the selected steps. Delete or Backspace clears the locks on every selected step. Clear Locks asks first, then clears the locks on every step and leaves the notes where they are. Clear Steps asks first, then switches every step off and sets each note to the key note, the one at or below the sample's root. The locks stay.

An LFO can live on steps. Press Map on the LFO while Lock is on and steps are selected, then click a knob. That LFO moves the knob only while those steps play, and it swings around the locked value when the step has one. The LFO panel shows it as → Knob on N steps. Its × removes that map from every step, and removes a live map of the same LFO as well. Double-clicking the knob in Lock removes the knob's lock and that knob's LFO map from the selected steps. Delete, Backspace and Clear Locks remove the map along with the other locks on those steps. A map you make with Lock off stays a live map, on every note. Envelopes stay live on every note, including while the sequencer plays.

4. The effects

The effects are strips in the order the sound passes through them: left to right, then the bottom row. Drag a strip by its grip, the dots at the top left, to move it. A bright line shows where it will land, and the sound follows when you let go. With a grip selected, the arrow keys move that strip one place. Esc while dragging puts it back. The order is remembered, and each take keeps the order it started with.

Each effect has an on/off. Off leaves the dry sound. Its reset button restores its knobs, switches and selectors to their defaults as one Undo step, while keeping its on/off state, step locks, and LFO and envelope maps. The Saturator is the bar along the bottom, labelled OUT. It always comes last, after whatever order you give the ten strips.

Knobs drag up to increase and down to decrease. Hold Shift for fine control. Double-click resets a knob. The scroll wheel and the arrow keys work too. In Lock, a double-click clears that knob's lock on the selected steps, as section 3 describes.

5. LFOs and envelopes

Six LFOs sit in the column next to the pad, on tabs 1 to 6. A light on a tab means that LFO is moving something. Each LFO drives one target.

Press Map. Knobs an LFO can drive get a glowing dashed outline, and the pad offers Pad X · pitch and Pad Y · rate. Click one. The panel shows where it went, for example → Filter · Cutoff. × unmaps it. Esc, or Map again, leaves map mode. Mapping a target another LFO already drives moves it to this LFO.

A mapped knob shows the LFO's number, a ring for the range it sweeps, and a dot for the live value. Turning the knob moves the centre the LFO swings around. With an LFO on Pad X or Pad Y, the cursor moves on its own, and a small circle shows where your hand is.

The LFOs keep their own time, so they drift apart. Reset restarts the one you are looking at. All restarts the six together. Those resets are recorded in a take and replay at the same moment.

Shape runs from sine through square to Random and Binary. Retrigger is Free (it keeps running), Touch (it restarts when you press the pad) or Note (it restarts on every arp note). Rate is in hertz, from 0.01 to 40, or synced to the tempo, from 8 bars down to 1/32. Depth, Offset, Phase, Jitter, Smooth, Min and Max shape the swing. Hold freezes the current value. Space Size, Decay and Damp stay unmapped, for the same reason a step leaves them alone: changing them rebuilds the reverb.

In Lock, with steps selected, Map stores the LFO on those steps instead of as a live map. Section 3 covers how that map shows up, and how × and a double-click remove it.

Two envelopes sit under the LFOs, on tabs 1 and 2. Attack rises, Decay falls to Sustain, and Release lets go. Amount sets how far and which way, from −100% to +100%. They start at 5 ms, 180 ms, 40%, 300 ms and +50%. Note restarts the envelope on every arp or sequencer note and lets go when the note ends. Touch starts it when you press the pad and lets go when you lift. With Hold on, Touch stays at Sustain.

Map, then click up to four knobs. Click a mapped knob again to remove it. A knob it drives shows E1 or E2. Both envelopes start off, and mapping a knob switches that envelope on. An envelope that is on and drives no knob shapes the loudness of each note instead. Where an LFO is also on that knob, the envelope adds on top. The picture shows the shape, with a dashed line at the live level.

6. Takes: record and re-render

Rec arms the recorder once a sample is loaded. The take starts on your next pad touch, or when you press play on the sequencer. Pressing Rec while the sequencer is already playing restarts it from step 1 and records from there.

Press Rec again to stop. The arp and the sequencer stop, and the reverb and delays ring out until they fall silent. The take then saves itself.

A take is a 24-bit WAV plus the performance: every pad move, knob turn, button and LFO change, and a copy of the sequencer pattern and the effect order from the moment the take started. Re-render plays that saved pattern and order. The log keeps the moves, knobs, buttons and LFO changes. Step edits made while recording stay out of it. The ⟳ button plays the performance again, in real time, through the sample, key and scale on screen now, and saves the result as a new take. Random parts of the sound, such as an Rnd pattern, grain spray and a random LFO, restart from the seed stored with the take, so the same settings come out the same.

Each row shows the length, key, scale and BPM, then a small waveform. Hover the details for the sample and its file name. The file name uses the arp's key and scale, as in adns-space_kick_124bpm_Amin_take03.wav. While a take plays, a playhead runs across its waveform, and clicking the waveform starts from that point. ▶ plays the WAV. In the Mac app, drag the take's name into a DAW, the Finder or a sampler to drop that WAV in, with no Save dialog. ⤓ downloads it. ✕ deletes it, and the second click confirms.

VOL, in the Out box, is the volume of your speakers, from 0 dB down to off. Double-click returns it to 0 dB. A take you play back goes through VOL too. The saved file, the download and the Out meter stay at the full level from before VOL. The app remembers VOL. Undo and Init keep it where it is.
